Rates used — 2026, BC, outside Quebec

 CPP: 5.95% on pensionable earnings, $3,500 basic exemption prorated per pay period, YMPE $74,600, max $4,230.45 each (employee + employer).
 CPP2: 4% on earnings $74,600–$85,000 (YAMPE), max $416 each.
 EI: 1.63% employee, up to $68,900 insurable, max $1,123.07 employee; employer 1.4× = 2.282%, max $1,572.30.
 Federal tax 2026: 14% to $58,523; 20.5% to $117,045; 26% to $181,440; 29% to $258,482; 33% above. Basic personal $16,452 (phases to $14,829 at high income — this app uses $16,452 / TD1 amount entered). Canada employment amount max $1,501, credit at 14% = $210.14.
 BC tax 2026: 5.6% to $50,363; 7.7% to $100,728; 10.5% to $115,648; 12.29% to $140,430; 14.7% to $190,405; 16.8% to $265,545; 20.5% above. Basic personal $13,216 at 5.6% = $740.10. BC tax reduction up to $690, clawed back 3.56% over $25,570 net income, gone at about $44,952.
 Method: income tax is annualised (this period’s gross × periods per year), run through the brackets, minus non-refundable credits (basic personal, Canada employment amount, base CPP and EI credits), then divided back by periods. That is the CRA table method in outline — it is NOT the full T4127 formula, and it does not include other TD1 credits, bonuses, taxable benefits, or province-of-employment changes. Verify against CRA PDOC before paying.
 NOT included here: BC Employer Health Tax (check if BC remuneration exceeds $500,000 — most small employers are below it), WorkSafeBC premiums (separate, by classification), and GST/HST (not a payroll deduction).
